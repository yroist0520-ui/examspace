There's a stress gap between men and women
A)
"I used to work very hard. I love to create things, grow them and solve problems," said Meng Li, a successful app developer in San Francisco. "I didn't really care about my mind and my body until they decided to go on strike."
B)
Ms. Li said her stress led to sleeplessness. When she did sleep, she experienced "problem-solving dreams," which left her feeling unrested when she woke up. "After I became a first-time mother, I quickly realized I was so busy caring for other people and work that I felt like I'd lost myself," she said.
C)
It's a common story—one we frequently ridicule and readily dismiss, for example, by claiming that women tend to complain more than men, despite the growing sum of research that underlines the problem. Women are twice as likely to suffer from severe stress and anxiety as men, according to a 2016 study published in The Journal of Brain & Behavior. The American Psychological Association reports a gender gap year after year showing that women consistently report higher stress levels. Clearly, a stress gap exists.
D)
"The difference is not really news to me, as a clinical psychologist," said Erin Joyce, a women and couples therapist in Los Angeles. "It's been well documented in extensive research over the years that prevalence rates for the majority of the anxiety disorders are higher in women than men." Some people may argue that this is merely reported data, and they say many men feel the same pressures as women in terms of fulfilling responsibilities at work and home. In other words, we're all really, really stressed.
E)
"The difference, however, is in the nature and scope of these responsibilities in the home environment in particular," Dr. Joyce said. For example, the United Nations reported that women do nearly three times as much unpaid domestic work as men. The problem is, housework is often overlooked as work, even though it is often as laborious (or in some cases, more so) as any paid job. As the scholar Silvia Federici put it in 1975, the unpaid nature of domestic work reinforces the assumption that "housework is not work, thus preventing women from struggling against it."
F)
It's not just inside the home, though. Research from Nova Southeastern University found that female managers were more likely than male managers to display "surface acting," or forcing emotions that are not wholly felt. "They expressed optimism, calmness and sympathy even when these were not the emotions that they were actually feeling," the study said.
G)
Surface acting is a prime example of "emotional labor," a concept that the writer Jess Zimmerman made familiar in a 2015 essay. The essay sparked a massive thread on the internet community blog MetaFilter. Hundreds of women spoke up about their own experience with emotional labor: the duties that are expected of them, but go unnoticed. These invisible duties become apparent only when you don't do them. Like domestic labor, emotional labor is generally dismissed and not labeled work. But research shows it can be just as exhausting as paid work. Emotional labor can lead to difficulty in sleeping and family conflict. Sure, circumstantial stress, like losing a job, may lead to these same issues. But emotional labor is not circumstantial. It's an enduring responsibility based on the socialized gender role of women.
H)
Like Ms. Li, many women try to manage the added stress to reach what Dr. Joyce said was an unattainable ideal. "Some professional women aim to do it all. They want to reach the top of the corporate ladder and fly like supermom," she said. When women don't reach this ideal, they feel guilty; and even more stressed. After her own struggle with this, Ms. Li took a step back and used her experience to build Sanity & Self, a self-care app and platform for overworked women. "The realizations I had in that process helped me gain insights and ultimately got me ready to integrate self-care into my daily life," she said.
I)
The stress problem extends beyond mental health when you consider the link between stress, anxiety and heart health. Worse, most of what we know about heart disease comes from studies involving men. However, "there are many reasons to think that it's different in women," Harvard Medical School reported. For example, women are more likely to experience disturbed sleep, anxiety and unusual fatigue before a heart attack. Stress is so normalized that it is easy for women to shrug off those symptoms as simply the consequences of stress. Many women also do not experience chest pain before a heart attack the way men do, which leads to fewer women discovering problematic heart issues. Harvard reports that women are "much more likely than men to die within a year of having a heart attack" and "many women say their physicians sometimes don't even recognize the symptoms."
J)
The good news is, women are more likely than men to take charge of their stress and manage it, the American Psychological Association reports. The concept of self-care, at its core, is quite simple. "The basics of adequate sleep, healthy diet and exercise are a good place to start," Dr. Joyce said. "Support from trusted relationships is vital. This includes professional support from various health and wellness providers if stress is becoming increasingly overwhelming."
K)
Disconnecting from work and home responsibilities is also obviously important. But it's much easier said than done. It is important to understand what causes your stress in the first place. "Get really specific with what's stressing you out," Ms. Li said. "We often chalk up our stress to broad experiences like work. But work stress can take many different forms. Is a colleague being disrespectful of your time? Is a boss undermining your day-to-day control over decision making? These are different causes of stress and can benefit from different kinds of self-care."
L)
Ideally, your spouse or partner will be supportive, rather than dismissive, of your stress. It is important to talk through these issues before they come to a head. "Women working outside of the home should make an effort to have a conscious conversation with their partners about more equitable sharing of household and family responsibilities," Dr. Joyce said.

Passage One
Questions 46 to 50 are based on the following passage.
As many office workers adapt to remote work, cities may undergo fundamental change if offices remain under-utilized. Who will benefit if working from home becomes the norm?
Employers argue they make considerable savings on real estate when workers shift from office to home work. However, these savings result from passing costs on to workers.
Unless employees are fully compensated, this could become a variant of parasitic(寄生的) capitalism, whereby corporate profits increasingly rely on extracting value from the public—and now personal—realm, rather than on generating new value.
Though employers are backed by a chorus of remote work advocates, others note the loneliness, reduced productivity and inefficiencies of extended remote work.
If working from home becomes permanent, employees will have to dedicate part of their private space to work. This requires purchasing desks, chairs and office equipment.
It also means having private space dedicated to work: the space must be heated, cleaned, maintained and paid for. That depends on many things, but for purposes of illustration, I have run some estimates for Montreal. The exercise is simple but important, since it brings these costs out of the realm of speculation into the realm of meaningful discussion.
Rough calculations show that the savings made by employers when their staff works from home are of similar value to the compensation workers should receive for setting up offices at home.
What does this mean for offices in cities? One of two things may happen: Employers pass these costs onto employees. This would be a form of expropriation(侵占), with employees absorbing production costs that have traditionally been paid by the employer. This represents a considerable transfer of value from employees to employers.
When employees are properly compensated, employers' real estate savings will be modest. If savings are modest, then the many advantages of working in offices—such as lively atmosphere, rapidity of communication, team-building and acclimatization(适应环境) of new employees—will encourage employers to shelve the idea of remote work and, like Yahoo in 2013, encourage employees to work most of the time from corporate office space.

Passage Two
Questions 51 to 55 are based on the following passage.
The human thirst for knowledge is the driving force behind our successful development as a species. But curiosity can also be dangerous, leading to setbacks or even downfalls. Given curiosity's complexity, scientists have found it hard to define.
While pinning down a definition has proven tricky, the general consensus is it's some means of information gathering. Psychologists also agree curiosity is intrinsically(内在地) motivated.
Curiosity covers such a large set of behaviors that there probably isn't any single "curiosity gene" that makes humans wonder about and explore their environment. That said, curiosity does have a genetic component. Genes and the environment interact in many complex ways to shape individuals and guide their behavior, including their curiosity.
Regardless of their genetic makeup, infants have to learn an incredible amount of information in a short time, and curiosity is one of the tools humans have found to accomplish that gigantic task.
Hundreds of studies show that infants prefer novelty. It's what motivates non-human animals, human infants and probably human adults to explore and seek out new things before growing less interested in them after continued exposure.
But curiosity often comes with a cost.
In some situations, the stakes are low and failure is a healthy part of growth. For instance, many babies are perfectly proficient crawlers, but they decide to try walking because there's more to see and do when they stand upright. But this milestone comes at a small cost. A study of 12- to 19-month-olds learning how to walk documented that these children fell down a lot. Seventeen times per hour, to be exact. But walking is faster than crawling, so this motivates expert crawlers to transition to walking.
Sometimes, however, testing out a new idea can lead to disaster. For instance, the Inuit people of the Arctic regions have created incredible modes to deal with the challenges of living in northern climates, but what we forget about are the tens of thousands of people that tried and failed to make it in those challenging landscapes.

FAQ · 常见问题

关于英语四级 2021年12月·第1套 真题（整卷）的常见问题

2021年12月英语四级真题第1套由哪几个部分、多少道题组成？
2021年12月英语四级真题第1套按四级标准结构分四大部分：写作1篇、听力理解8个模块共25题、阅读理解（选词填空1篇10空、长篇阅读信息匹配1篇10题、仔细阅读四选一2篇共10题）、翻译1篇（汉译英）。听力占25题，阅读客观题30个（含选词填空10空），加上写作、翻译两道主观题，构成完整一套四级卷。
2021年12月英语四级真题第1套听力部分具体考了哪些题型？
2021年12月四级第1套听力共8个模块25题，分三节：Section A 是3篇新闻报道（Q1-7，每篇后2-3题）、Section B 是2篇长对话（Q8-15，每篇后4题）、Section C 是3篇短文讲座（Q16-25，每篇后3-4题）。八个模块的听力英文原文在懒笔记听力详情页均可逐句对照阅读。
2021年12月英语四级真题第1套的阅读文章难度分布如何？
2021年12月四级第1套共4篇阅读文章，按CEFR难度为3篇B2、1篇C1，四篇合计约1940词。其中C1难度的是仔细阅读第一篇《远程办公》(Remote Work)，另外三篇（选词填空、长篇匹配、仔细阅读好奇心篇）均为B2，整体处于四级中上难度区间。
2021年12月英语四级真题第1套哪篇阅读文章篇幅最长、各篇多少词？
2021年12月四级第1套篇幅最长的是长篇阅读信息匹配《男女之间存在压力鸿沟》，约1030词；两篇仔细阅读分别约338词（远程办公）和335词（人类为何好奇）；选词填空短文约237词。四篇阅读合计约1940词。
2021年12月英语四级真题第1套听力篇章的高频实义词是哪些？
2021年12月四级第1套各听力篇章核心词非常集中：讲捕猎鸭子的长对话里 duck 出现17次、hunting 7次；讲房屋粉刷的长对话里 paint 出现10次、furniture 6次；讲减肥奖金的短文里 weight、healthy、worker 各出现约7-9次。抓住这些反复出现的关键词即可快速锁定每段听力主题。
2021年12月英语四级真题第1套翻译考的是什么主题？
2021年12月四级第1套翻译（汉译英）考的是大运河（Grand Canal）。原文介绍大运河是世界上最长的人工河，北起北京、南至杭州，始建于公元前4世纪、公元13世纪末建成，最初用于运输粮食、后来运送其他商品，沿线逐渐发展成中国工商业中心，并促进了南北人员往来与文化交流。同期第2、3套翻译分别考都江堰和坎儿井，三套同属中国古代水利工程主题。
2021年12月英语四级真题第1套作文题目是什么？
2021年12月四级第1套写作要求写一份提案（proposal）：假设学校要组织一个迎新项目（orientation program）帮助大一新生适应新环境和学业，考生需就该项目的目标、时长、参与者和活动内容写一份提案，限时30分钟、120-180词，属于应用文中的提案类写作。
2021年12月英语四级真题第1套长篇阅读（信息匹配）讲的是什么？
2021年12月四级第1套长篇阅读信息匹配的文章题为《男女之间存在压力鸿沟》(There's a Stress Gap Between Men and Women)，选自纽约时报，全文约1030词。文章围绕女性比男性承受更多压力与焦虑展开：谈到无偿家务劳动、“情绪劳动”(emotional labor)、压力与心脏健康的关联，以及自我关怀(self-care)的重要性。
2021年12月英语四级真题第1套两篇仔细阅读（四选一）分别讲什么？
2021年12月四级第1套仔细阅读两篇：第一篇（Q46-50）《远程办公》探讨在家办公若成为常态谁真正受益，指出雇主省下的房地产成本实际被转嫁给员工，属C1难度、抽象词较多；第二篇（Q51-55）《人类为何如此好奇》讨论好奇心既是人类发展的驱动力、也可能带来风险，以婴儿学步、北极因纽特人为例说明好奇的代价。
2021年12月英语四级真题第1套选词填空的短文主题是什么？
2021年12月四级第1套选词填空短文主题是“谦逊”(humility)与恋爱关系，约237词，选自 theconversation.com。核心观点是谦逊是维系长久恋情的重要指标——谦逊的人能坦诚看待自己的不足与长处，反而更容易开启并维持一段浪漫关系。
2021年12月英语四级真题第1套听力三篇新闻报道分别报道了什么？
2021年12月四级第1套听力Section A三篇新闻：第一篇讲美联航(United Airlines)误把一家美国人的宠物狗Buddy运错方向、送到了日本东京；第二篇讲美国亚利桑那州图森市Reid Park动物园一头小象诞生（母象Semba，幼象重约130公斤）；第三篇讲一对夫妇三年前在海边餐厅丢失的结婚戒指，被餐厅更换木板时发现并寄还，同时发现一枚1855年的稀有金币。
2021年12月英语四级真题第1套听力长对话和短文讲了哪些话题？
2021年12月四级第1套听力Section B两篇长对话分别讨论捕猎鸭子在多国仍属合法、以及鸭子数量因干旱与气候变化而下降，另一篇是一位女士为房屋粉刷询价（总价约3000美元、需搬家并耗时两天）；Section C三篇短文分别讲小学生做作业的目的与家长如何辅导、墨西哥某石油公司按体重发放健康奖金引发的争议、以及英国政府因未帮助左撇子学生而受到批评。
2021年12月英语四级真题第1套的答案和解析在哪里看？
2021年12月四级第1套官方从不公布标准答案，网络上流传的是新东方、沪江、文都等机构的参考答案，各家在个别选择题上偶有出入，仅供核对参考。想系统对照的话，懒笔记整卷页按真实卷面排版收录了第1套的写作、听力、阅读、翻译全部题目，并提供题目解析与原文精读，可逐题查看。
2021年12月英语四级真题第1套难不难？
2021年12月四级第1套整体难度属中上：4篇阅读中3篇B2、1篇C1，其中远程办公篇最难，含 parasitic capitalism、expropriation 等抽象词汇；翻译大运河涉及“人工河、公元前4世纪、工商业中心”等表达，比同期都江堰、坎儿井两套略常规。听力三篇新闻话题生活化（宠物狗、小象、结婚戒指），相对好把握。
2021年12月英语四级真题第1套的听力原文和翻译参考译文哪里找？
2021年12月四级第1套8个听力模块的完整英文原文，可在懒笔记听力详情页逐句对照查看（含新闻、长对话、短文全文）；翻译“大运河”的参考译文可在整卷页翻译板块查看，核心句如“世界上最长的人工河”通常译为 the longest man-made river in the world。需注意听力原文以官方录音为准，参考译文为机构版本、并非唯一标准。
2021年12月英语四级第1套和第2套、第3套有什么区别？
2021年12月四级三套卷结构相同、题目不同，最明显区别在翻译与阅读话题：第1套翻译考大运河、第2套考都江堰、第3套考坎儿井，三者同属中国古代水利工程主题。第1套的阅读文章为“男女压力鸿沟”“远程办公”“人类为何好奇”“谦逊与恋爱”，与另两套不重合。核对答案时务必按套号对照，避免张冠李戴。
